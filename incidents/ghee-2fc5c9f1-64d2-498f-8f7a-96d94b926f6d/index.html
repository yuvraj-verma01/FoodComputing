Article text

Cleaned research copy

Bengaluru: The state's iconic Nandini ghee, produced by Karnataka Milk Federation (KMF), has begun its overseas journey with exports to Australia, the US and Saudi Arabia. Chief minister Siddaramaiah flagged off the first consignment Monday, noting that demand for the ghee has grown significantly, both domestically and internationally. The export push comes on the back of a surge in institutional and retail demand. Siddaramaiah praised KMF and US-based Mysuru native Kumar, who has secured the export agency for Nandini ghee, saying their partnership would help expand the brand's footprint abroad. KMF has supplied a record 5,000 tonnes of ghee to Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams (TTD) over the past year for use in the famous laddu prasadam â a rise of more than 30% compared to previous years. TTD's renewed tie-up with KMF followed quality concerns linked to adulterated ghee from other vendors, leading to Nandini's reinstatement as the exclusive supplier. Rising demand has also attracted illicit activity. Bengaluru police recently busted a counterfeit racket involving fake Nandini pouches filled with adulterated ghee, seizing 8,136 litres of tainted stock. Meanwhile, KMF increased retail price of ghee by Rs 90 per kg earlier this month, citing global production costs. The new price of Rs 700 per litre has sparked concerns among consumers. Nandini milk and powder is already being exported to Australia, France, UK, Germany and Saudi Arabia in tune with global standards. The federation, which produces more than 11.5 million litres of milk a day, is channelling surplus volumes to international markets. KMF marked a major production milestone in May 2025 when it collected 1 crore litres of milk in a single day. Analysts say this capacity supports both domestic supply and the federation's new export commitments. To sustain overseas shipments, KMF is aligning with global hygiene norms, improving packaging, expanding cold-chain logistics and strengthening certification systems.

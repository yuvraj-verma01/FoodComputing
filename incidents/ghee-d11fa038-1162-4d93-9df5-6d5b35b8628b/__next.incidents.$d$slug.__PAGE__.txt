6a:Ta3e,Hyderabad: An investigation by food safety authorities into purity of ghee available in the market has revealed that one out of every five samples tested was either substandard or unsafe for consumption.
Out of the 70 samples collected randomly between January and September from various sources, including dairy farms, supermarkets and stores, 16 were found to be adulterated after multiple tests such as Baudouin, butyro refractometer reading, chemical and fat content analysis.
The tests assessed the purity of ghee by checking the fat percentage, presence of contaminants and existence of adulterants such as vanaspati hydrogenated fat, vegetable oil and animal fats.
"The tests were performed to ensure that the ghee samples met the required standards. During the testing process, we discovered adulterated ghee samples containing palm oil and vegetable fat, which compromised the purity and authenticity of the ghee, making it substandard and potentially harmful for consumption," said a senior food safety official.
The price of ghee in the market varies significantly, ranging from Rs 300 to Rs 1,000 per kg, based on purity. 
Although, only around 20% was discovered to be adulterated, most of the low-priced loosely sold products are not genuine, officials said.
Health experts too expressed concern about the practice of adulterating ghee with less expensive oils and fats. 
Consuming ghee with altered fatty acid composition can lead to an imbalance in essential nutrients. This alteration not only increases the caloric content but also contributes to various health issues such as obesity, high cholesterol levels, and elevated blood pressure, doctors said.
"The presence of adulterants in ghee causes digestive problems, including bloating and stomach discomfort. Regular consumption of adulterated ghee can have detrimental effects on the immune system, leading to chronic inflammation and oxidative stress within the body. If taken regularly, consumers will be at risk of cardiovascular disease, cancers, and neurological disorders in the long term," said Deepa Agarwal, founder of Nutriclinic Hyderabad.
Over 15,000 kg loose butter seized

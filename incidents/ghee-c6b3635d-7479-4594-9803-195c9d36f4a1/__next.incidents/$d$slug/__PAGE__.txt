35:T9ed,Ludhiana: Food safety authorities in this city have seized about 50 kg of alleged adulterated desi ghee and raw materials following a major raid on a house operating as an illicit production unit.
The raid, conducted on Monday in the city's Sham Nagar area, also netted cream and milk powder believed to be used in the production of the substandard ghee, the food safety department confirmed. The operation was initiated based on a confidential tip. One woman connected to the case was taken into custody with the support of the Bus Stand police post team. "All the material was immediately confiscated, and a detailed investigation into the matter has been initiated," the department said in a statement issued here.
Repeated Violations
Preliminary inquiries revealed that the individuals involved in the illegal production are repeat offenders who have faced legal action under the Food Safety and Standards Act in 2017 and 2020 for similar activities. In a further sign of the persistent nature of the illegal operation, the suspects were declared absconders by the court in 2023, the department noted. "This shows clearly that these persons had been endangering public health repeatedly," the department added.
Samples Sent to Lab
The Food Safety team collected samples of the ghee, cream, and milk powder from the site. These have been sent to a laboratory for testing. Food Safety Officers Yogesh Goyal, Divyajot Kaur, and Harsimran Kaur successfully conducted the operation in coordination with the local police. "Once the reports are received, strict legal action will be taken against the individuals under the Food Safety and Standards Act," the department stated. 
Zero-Tolerance Policy
Ludhiana civil surgeon, Dr Ramandeep Kaur, emphasised the serious public health threat posed by consuming food products made with adulterated ingredients, particularly during the ongoing festive season. She warned that the use of low-quality materials not only endangers public health but also places a significant strain on the healthcare system. She affirmed that the food safety department and police had adopted a "zero-tolerance policy" against this kind of illegal activities.
Dr Ramandeep Kaur appealed to the public to exercise caution, advising them to only purchase sweets, ghee, and other food items from licensed shops. She urged consumers to "check the quality, standards, and packaging carefully before purchasing".

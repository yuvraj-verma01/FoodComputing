49:T96d,Fake Nandini ghee racket busted in Bengaluru; dip in orders exposed accused
Karnataka Milk Federation officials busted a fake ghee racket run by an authorised distributor in Bengaluru. The distributor allegedly diluted genuine Nandini ghee with palm oil and sold it to parlours.
A fake ghee racket supplying products to Nandini dairy parlours across Bengaluru has been busted, with officials uncovering an adulteration network run by an authorised distributor.
Investigations revealed that the group produced adulterated ghee in Tamil Nadu, filled it into counterfeit Nandini sachets and plastic bottles, and supplied it to accused in Bengaluru who held official Karnataka Milk Federation (KMF) licences. These distributors then sold the fake ghee to wholesale shops, retail outlets, and Nandini parlours at full market price.
SUSPICIOUSLY REDUCED ORDER
The scam came to light after KMF officials noticed something unusual: Mahendra, an official KMF dealer-cum-distributor who regularly placed large ghee orders, suddenly began cutting down his purchases. Instead of his usual 100 litres, he had been ordering just 50 litres in recent months, raising red flags.
Inspections revealed why. Mahendra, the key accused, had allegedly been mixing palm oil and other fats with genuine Nandini ghee, turning 1 litre of real ghee into 5 litres of adulterated product. This fake ghee was then supplied to Nandini parlours across the city.
A joint operation by the City Crime Branch (CCB) Special Investigation Squad and KMF Vigilance Wing, acting on secretly gathered intelligence, tracked the network.
On Friday, teams raided godowns, shops, and goods vehicles belonging to Krishna Enterprises in Chamarajpet, owned by Mahendra, and his family. A vehicle transporting adulterated ghee was seized and the driver arrested.
KMF Vigilance later expanded the probe, exposing the full scope of Mahendra’s operation. According to sources, he allegedly resorted to adulteration to raise money for his daughter’s wedding.

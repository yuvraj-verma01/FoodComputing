Laboratory tests found hundreds of unsafe and substandard food samples, raising concerns over enforcement and consumer safety.

Guwahati, June 16: A series of food safety enforcement drives across Assam over the past one month has uncovered widespread use of banned chemicals and substandard food products in local markets, raising concerns about the effectiveness of existing regulations and the safety of food reaching consumers.

Officials from the Food Safety Wing (FSW) have conducted multiple inspections in different districts, repeatedly detecting the illegal use of calcium carbide, a prohibited chemical commonly used to artificially ripen fruits.

On June 6, the FSW carried out an enforcement drive at Beltola Market in Guwahati, where officials detected the illegal use of calcium carbide. Formal samples were collected for laboratory analysis, while nearly 100 kg of chemically ripened mangoes were seized and handed over to the Guwahati Municipal Corporation (GMC) for immediate disposal.

The same day, authorities conducted an inspection of cheese products in Kamrup district to verify compliance with food safety standards. During the drive, officials found cheese allegedly prepared using skimmed milk powder (SMP) and refined palmolein oil. Around 20 kg of the product were seized and legal procedures initiated under the Food Safety and Standards Act, 2006.

Earlier on June 4, officials detected the use of calcium carbide for ripening bananas at Barpeta Road. The entire stock was destroyed at the municipal dumping ground, and samples of both the chemical and the fruit were collected for laboratory testing and further legal action.

A day earlier, on June 3, a surprise surveillance drive in Fancy Bazar, Guwahati uncovered another case of artificial ripening of mangoes using calcium carbide. The entire stock was destroyed at the GMC dumping ground, while samples were sent for analysis.

On June 1, authorities reportedly destroyed an entire truckload of vegetables suspected to contain harmful chemical residue at Barpeta Road. Earlier, on May 25, around 200 kg of chemically ripened bananas were also destroyed during enforcement operations.

The broader picture is far more alarming. The State Public Health Laboratory here tested some 5,166 food samples during the financial year 2025-26, of which 177 were found to be unsafe and 531 substandard.

Supari, catechu, pan masala and mouth fresheners topped the list of unsafe food, with a total of 98 out of 109 samples tested found to be unsafe or substandard. Six out of 20 packaged drinking water bottles, 20 out of 180 spice powders, 235 out of 1,143 cereals and cereal products, and 65 out of 750 edible and mustard oil products were found unsafe or substandard.

Worse, harmful pesticide residue were detected in 19 out of 83 fresh vegetables tested at the laboratory. Also, around 138 butter, paneer, cheese and other milk products were tested at the laboratory and 28 were either unsafe or substandard.

Despite repeated crackdowns, substandard and potentially harmful food products continue to find their way into markets, leaving consumers uncertain about how to identify safe and healthy produce.

The recurring violations have also sparked questions about whether the current frequency of inspections and enforcement drives is sufficient to tackle the problem effectively. Food safety officials acknowledge that while enforcement actions are being intensified, there remains scope to strengthen existing laws and penalties, which, critics argue, have failed to serve as an adequate deterrent against food adulteration and the illegal use of ripening agents.

Around 16 posts of district food safety officers are currently lying vacant in the State, and some officers are holding additional charges. Penalties are imposed on traders dealing with substandard foods, while those selling unsafe food are prosecuted. However, the legal proceedings take considerable time, sources said.

41:T5dd,The toxin in the contaminated milk, which led to the death of five persons, and hospitalisation of 15 others, in Rajamahendravaram, is likely to identified in a few days, announced Dr. T. Ravi Raju, noted nephrologist, and former Vice-Chancellor of Dr. NTR University of Health Sciences (UHS).

Dr. Ravi Raju was consulted by the government of Andhra Pradesh, after the consumption of the contaminated milk in Rajamahendravaram on February 16, led to anuria (lack of urine) and hospitalisation of 20 persons, of whom five have died.

"The blood and urine samples of the affected persons have been sent to reputed testing centres for identification of the toxin, and the results are expected in a few days," he said.

"On that day (February 16), several persons had consumed the adulterated milk. Though the milk left a bad taste, elderly persons did not want to waste it and consumed it, not knowing that it could lead to serious health issues. The youngsters declined to drink due to the bad taste, while some infants were force-fed. Those who drank the milk suffered abdominal pain and subsequently their kidneys stopped producing urine. They suffered breathlessness and were put on ventilators. " Dr. Ravi Raju told The Hindu, when contacted on Friday.

"I am guiding the local doctors. The district administration responded promptly. I have already gone there (Rajamahendravaram) twice, and I will go again to supervise their treatment," Dr. Raju added.
